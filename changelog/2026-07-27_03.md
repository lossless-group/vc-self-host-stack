## Why Care?

Every Lossless stack sells the same promise: your tools, your data, and an
agent that can reach all of it through APIs. That third clause is a hard
requirement, and today it disqualified a tool we'd already deployed. Docmost
is arguably the most polished self-hosted Notion alternative — and its
self-hosted edition greys out API keys unless you buy an Enterprise license.
Michael hit the gate in the settings screen within hours of the deploy.
No API, no agent, no deal.

The evaluation cost us almost nothing — the per-client deploy discipline
meant standing Docmost up took one pass, and tearing it down took minutes.
Cheap experiments are the point of the pattern.

## Under the Hood — the Railway redeploy trap

Docmost's exit was hastened by a debugging session that produced the most
useful finding of the day: **`railway redeploy` re-runs the PREVIOUS
deployment's spec.** Start-command and config changes made after a deploy
are silently dropped by redeploys — only a NEW deployment (cheapest trigger:
set a throwaway `CONFIG_EPOCH` variable without skipping deploys) picks
them up. Variables, by contrast, ARE re-resolved on redeploy, which is why
our Twenty deploys *seemed* to confirm redeploy worked.

We proved it with an in-network diagnostic service that interrogated the
live redis: bone-stock config, 204 of 204 background saves failed, every
write rejected — while the service config page showed the "applied" start
command the process never received. The finding is now stamped into both
the Twenty and Outline runbooks.
